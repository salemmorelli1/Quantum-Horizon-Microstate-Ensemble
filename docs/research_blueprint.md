# Differentiable Quantum-Horizon Area Ensembles

## 1. Physical formulation

For fixed puncture number `N`, the discrete spin space is

\[
\mathcal J^N=\{1/2,1,3/2,\ldots\}^N,
\]

with area

\[
A(\mathbf j)=C\sum_i\sqrt{j_i(j_i+1)},
\qquad C=8\pi\gamma\ell_p^2.
\]

The continuous relaxation is

\[
x_i=\frac12+\operatorname{softplus}(z_i),
\qquad z_i\in\mathbb R.
\]

Define

\[
F(\mathbf x)=
\frac{\kappa_A}{2}(\widetilde A-A_\star)^2
+\kappa_Q\sum_i\sin^2(2\pi x_i)
-\eta\sum_i\log(2x_i+1).
\]

If the proxy density is proportional to `exp(-F(x)) dx`, the latent potential is

\[
U(\mathbf z)=F(\mathbf x(\mathbf z))-\sum_i\log\sigma(z_i).
\]

The Jacobian is mandatory. Without it, `U` approaches a finite limit as one
`z_i` approaches negative infinity, producing an improper latent distribution.

## 2. Gradient geometry

Let

\[
r=\widetilde A-A_\star,
\qquad
a'(x)=\frac{2x+1}{2\sqrt{x(x+1)}}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_i}
=\kappa_A C r a'(x_i)
+2\pi\kappa_Q\sin(4\pi x_i)
-\frac{2\eta}{2x_i+1}.
\]

With `q_i = sigmoid(z_i)`, the complete latent gradient is

\[
\frac{\partial U}{\partial z_i}
=q_i\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_i}+q_i-1.
\]

The repository evaluates this field with `torch.autograd.grad` and independently
checks it against the closed form.

## 3. Comb concentration

The periodic term satisfies

\[
\sin^2(2\pi x)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z.
\]

Near a half-integer `h`,

\[
\sin^2(2\pi(h+\delta))=4\pi^2\delta^2+O(\delta^4).
\]

Thus the local comb curvature is `8 pi^2 kappa_Q`, and the approximate
within-well variance is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\delta)\approx(8\pi^2\kappa_Q)^{-1}.
\]

For every fixed positive distance from the lattice, the comb is bounded below
by a positive constant on compact area sublevels. The off-lattice probability
therefore decays exponentially with `kappa_Q`, up to the polynomial Laplace
factor generated by neighborhoods of the lattice minima.

This proves concentration, not exact discreteness. The area and degeneracy
forces also shift finite-`kappa_Q` modes by order `1/kappa_Q`.

## 4. Hamiltonian sampler

The Hamiltonian is

\[
H(\mathbf z,\mathbf p)=U(\mathbf z)+\frac12\mathbf p^TM^{-1}\mathbf p.
\]

Each leapfrog step performs

\[
\mathbf p_{n+1/2}=\mathbf p_n-\frac\epsilon2\nabla U(\mathbf z_n),
\]

\[
\mathbf z_{n+1}=\mathbf z_n+\epsilon M^{-1}\mathbf p_{n+1/2},
\]

\[
\mathbf p_{n+1}=\mathbf p_{n+1/2}-\frac\epsilon2\nabla U(\mathbf z_{n+1}).
\]

The numerical trajectory is followed by the acceptance probability

\[
\alpha=\min\{1,\exp[-H_{\mathrm{proposal}}+H_{\mathrm{current}}]\}.
\]

Step-size and diagonal-mass adaptation occur only during warmup. Integration
length and step size are randomized to reduce fixed-period resonances.

## 5. Statistical geometry

In proxy-spin coordinates,

\[
\nabla_x^2F
=\kappa_A C^2\mathbf v\mathbf v^T+\operatorname{diag}(d_i),
\]

where `v_i = a'(x_i)`. This identifies one collective area direction and `N-1`
predominantly tangential microstate directions.

For `N > 1000`, the recommended metric is

\[
M=D+\rho\mathbf u\mathbf u^T,
\]

where `u` is aligned with the latent area gradient. Its inverse can be applied
in linear time using Sherman–Morrison.

## 6. Ergodicity and metastability

The corrected target is smooth, proper, positive, and supported on connected
latent space. Gaussian momentum refreshment and randomized trajectories help
exploration; connected support and numerical acceptance alone do not prove
global equilibration or adequate mixing among spin sectors.

Nevertheless, adjacent comb wells are separated by a barrier of height
`kappa_Q` per changed coordinate. Area-preserving microstate changes may require
several coordinated spin changes, so inter-well mixing can still deteriorate
rapidly with dimension.

The confirmatory sampler should therefore add a replica ladder

\[
\pi_r(\mathbf x)\propto
\exp\{-U_{\mathrm{macro}}-U_{\mathrm{deg}}
-\tau_r\kappa_QQ(\mathbf x)\},
\]

with `0 = tau_0 < ... < tau_R = 1`. The flat-comb replicas explore spin sectors,
while exchanges transmit those configurations to the target replica.

The current implementation is single-chain HMC. It does not implement NUTS,
replica exchange, rank-normalized multichain R-hat, or bulk/tail ESS. Its scalar
ESS uses a conservative initial monotone autocorrelation sequence; short and
constant traces are reported as unidentified. See the scientific audit for
the schema-2 provenance and sampling-status contract.

## 7. Entropy boundary

HMC draws from a normalized density and does not directly return a microstate
count. With

\[
Z(\kappa_Q)=\int e^{-F_0(\mathbf x)-\kappa_QQ(\mathbf x)}d\mathbf x,
\]

thermodynamic integration uses

\[
\frac{d}{d\kappa_Q}\log Z(\kappa_Q)
=-\mathbb E_{\kappa_Q}[Q(\mathbf X)].
\]

Absolute entropy additionally requires a calibrated reference normalizer and an
exact discrete degeneracy correction. The physical invariant-space multiplicity
is

\[
d_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mathbf j)=
\dim\operatorname{Inv}_{SU(2)}\left(\bigotimes_iV_{j_i}\right),
\]

not simply `prod_i(2j_i + 1)`.

## 8. Falsification program

1. Enumerate small-`N` lattice ensembles exactly.
2. Compare relaxed, rounded, and corrected probabilities.
3. Verify lattice distance decreases at the predicted comb-curvature rate.
4. Scale `N` through 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, and 4096.
5. Report ESS per gradient, rank-normalized R-hat, divergences, energy error,
   replica swaps, area RMSE, and spin-sector transitions.
6. Compare factorized, surrogate-intertwiner, and exact-corrected degeneracies.
7. Fit entropy only after the normalizing-constant estimator passes simulation-
   based calibration on exactly enumerable systems.
